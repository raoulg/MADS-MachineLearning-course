Your study, as a loop

Every lesson has a folder exercises/ for a study with a design of your own: one idea, a design that can test it, a search, and a result the class can build on. Every loop starts from an idea of the class and ends in one. The loop is the same in every lesson; what a lesson brings to it, its data, its baseline and its notebook, stands under Per lesson.

your design: designs.pyyour study: study.pythe class

Part 1 of the lesson's exercise notebook walks steps 3 to 6 once, with a design and a space that are given, and its runs are handed in with yours in step 8. Run it first: it measures your baseline and what one trial costs on your machine, which steps 3 and 4 here use. The folder exercises/ walks the whole loop, with a design of your own. Which notebook that is stands under Per lesson.

The nine steps

  1. 1Design

    Sketch the blocks as typed stages: a Config with the choices, one method per stage, and a forward that calls them in order. Read the shape on every arrow in atlas before you train anything. A block the search may leave out is a choice, not a second model. Your first design builds on the exercise as handed out: IDEA names that idea in builds_on.

    You decide which blocks the model has, in which order, what shape each takes and gives, and which of them the search may leave out.

    designs.py · Config
    goals 1.122.72.10

  2. 2Build

    Fill the stages in. An optional stage carries @option("<axis>"), has a field in Config, and runs under if self.config.<axis>: in forward. The file ends in DESIGN = Design(...), and its last line, DESIGN.show(example, ...), builds every combination of the options and prints the shape after every stage.

    You decide the name of each optional stage's axis, and whether the shapes DESIGN.show prints are the ones your sketch promised.

    designs.py · @option · DESIGN
    goal 2.10

  3. 3Baseline

    A linear model, repeated, so you know its spread between runs. study.py measures it before the search, on the data your search is scored on. A difference smaller than that spread is not a difference.

    You decide nothing yet: you read how far apart two runs of the same model land, and what one trial costs on your machine.

    study.py · Part 1 of the exercise notebook
    goal 1.13

  4. 4Budget and space

    Your minutes on your machine become trials. Choose the axes those trials can cover; every option of the design is an axis, searched or fixed. Say in EXPECTS what you expect, and why, before you run.

    You decide the name of the study, the idea in your own words and what it builds on, what you expect and why, the metric the search maximises, the axes and their ranges, and the minutes you spend.

    study.py: STUDY · IDEA · EXPECTS · OBJECTIVE · SPACE · MINUTES
    goal 1.14

  5. 5Search

    An Optuna study over your space and your design, then the winner rerun as often as the baseline. The script refuses a space that leaves out an option or holds an axis that neither the design nor the trainer knows, and a study that already holds trials of another space.

    You decide what to change when the script refuses: the space, the design, or the name of the study.

    study.py · your Optuna store
    goal 1.14

  6. 6Read

    Per axis: what each did to your objective and to train_seconds. Every run logs more than the objective, for a question you ask later. The winner in sigmas against the baseline: inside about two, the difference is not a result, whatever its sign.

    You decide what the study says about your idea, set against what you wrote in EXPECTS.

    OBJECTIVE · train_seconds · the Optuna dashboard
    goal 1.10

  7. 7Explain

    Your page of the lesson, built with make page LESSON=<N>: what you show in class when Raoul walks past and has three minutes. The template fills what your store holds: the line of your ideas, the architecture, the search space, the winner next to its baseline. The message, a picture, what surprised you and what comes next are yours, in exercises/page.toml. Your agent asks whether you understand your own page, and writes down your answers in your words. You have done DAV: use it, the first impression should be the message. The beelddenker skill helps your assistant turn an answer into a picture.

    You decide the three sentences that say what you did, and whether a picture carries them. The test: can you explain your page in three sentences, without looking?

    make page · exercises/page.toml: message · picture · surprised · next
    goals 1.102.11

  8. 8Share

    Your runs and your design script go to the class. Every run of the study carries designs.py, so the class sees the architecture next to the numbers. Handing in follows docs/submit-runs.md.

    You decide whether the list of runs that will be handed in is the one you mean to hand in.

    your runs · designs.py · docs/submit-runs.md
    goals 1.152.12

  9. 9Build on

    Pick an idea of the class as the parent of your next one: its name (blissful-bee-18) goes in builds_on. A new idea is a new STUDY, and you defend its design without your agent. The page of a rehearsal is an example of a class page: what it looks like after three rounds, four made-up students, six ideas, each arrow the gain of an idea over the one it builds on.

    You decide which idea of the class your next one builds on, and what you expect it to add.

    builds_on · STUDY · the class page
    goals 1.152.112.12

The files

exercises/designs.py
Your design: Config, the choices, and the supergraph with one typed stage per operation. Its comments say how to add a stage of your own.
exercises/study.py
Your study: the six settings at the top, then one hpo.exercise.Exercise that runs it: baseline, budget, search, winner and the difference in sigmas, the same in every lesson.
exercises/harness.py
What the lesson gives the study: its data, the trainer, the linear baseline and the params a run logs. It trains whatever make_model(config, data) builds.

How it counts

Your research is assessed in the lesson, along five criteria that weigh the same: the overview at a glance, the architecture, the search space, the results, and the code. The rubric stands on learn.pttrn.io. Your research gives a bonus of at most 0.5 on the grade of the final assignment, and the bonus only counts when that grade is above 5.5. Lesson 7 is the last lesson in which you can have your research assessed.

folder
notebooks/3_scaling_and_search/exercises/
notebook
Part 1 of 05_exercise.ipynb
data
Lesson 2's heartbeats: MIT-BIH, split by patient, three classes, N, S and V. train_fraction takes a stratified share of the training beats, so data is an axis of the search.
design
Beats in designs.py, with the route stage @option("route"), a mixture of experts on every position of the shortened beat, and its control of the same parameter count, @option("matched").
baseline
A linear model on the 187 steps of a beat, repeated, so you know its spread between runs. study.py measures it before the search, on the six validation patients your search is scored on.
space
The capacity axes: parameters (width, depth), data (train_fraction, with equal_steps to hold the steps) and compute (epochs). With "route": True and matched searched, every draw trains the route stage and its control as a pair, on the same seed.
metrics
Every run logs its whole confusion matrix, and every run with a router the use of its experts (expert_share, expert_weight, expert_peak, experts_in_use), for a question you ask later.
harness
What lesson 3 gives the study: the heartbeats, train_fraction, the trainer, the linear baseline, the pairing and the readouts (the paired curves, the use of the experts, the control at the winner). study.py runs RoutedExercise, hpo.exercise.Exercise with paired trials.
